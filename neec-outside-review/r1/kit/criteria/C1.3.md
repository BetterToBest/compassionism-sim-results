# C1.3 Housing Security

**Requirement.** ≥88% housing stability resistant to market volatility, with housing affordable to households at 80% of the regional median disposable income.

**Pass threshold.** ≥88% housing stability over 5-year periods, housing costs ≤30% of disposable income at 80% of regional median household disposable income

**Measurement.** Housing stability rate = percentage of participants maintaining stable housing over 5-year periods without involuntary displacement from foreclosure, eviction, or inability to afford rent increases. Affordability: housing costs (rent or mortgage payments plus utilities) no more than 30% of disposable income for a household at 80% of the regional median household disposable income, from the OECD Affordable Housing Database, EU-SILC or national household surveys; for a design, projected on the same measure.

**Indicators.** household panel surveys for displacement; OECD Affordable Housing Database; EU-SILC; national household surveys.

**Rationale.** Housing represents the largest expense (30-50% of income) and primary wealth-building mechanism. Systems failing to address housing commodification cannot claim comprehensive security. Stability means maintaining housing over 5+ years without involuntary displacement.

**Benchmark.** Community Land Trusts achieve 10x lower foreclosure rates than conventional mortgages (0.46% vs 3.26% during 2008-2010 crisis).

**Derivation.** From N1 (Human Flourishing) + N3 (Coercion Minimization)

**Pass Threshold, clause by clause** (all must be shown cleared for 1.0; protocol 2.3):

1. ≥88% housing stability over 5-year periods
2. housing costs ≤30% of disposable income at 80% of regional median household disposable income

**Note.** Examples are re-estimated in the rescoring pass.

**Anchor bands** (they describe; the Pass Threshold governs, protocol 4.6):

- **1.0** Structural decommodification or strong regulatory stabilization with a documented or clearly-designed stability rate at or above threshold.
- **0.5** Partial protection or a real but incomplete mechanism — e.g., some cooperative/social housing alongside a still-dominant market allocation.
- **0.0** Pure market allocation with no stabilizing mechanism.
