# C1.4 Automation Resilience

**Requirement.** Maintain material security as human labor becomes increasingly optional: poverty <8% and aggregate demand >85% of baseline in each of three scenarios in which 30%, 50% and 70% of baseline paid working hours are displaced by automation.

**Pass threshold.** Poverty <8% and aggregate demand >85% baseline in each of the 30%, 50% and 70% displacement scenarios

**Clause scope.** in each of the 30%, 50% and 70% displacement scenarios

**Measurement.** Stress test across the three automation scenarios, a displacement of 30%, 50% or 70% of baseline paid working hours, with no assumption about when each occurs. In each, poverty is measured at the Societal Poverty Line, as in C1.1, and aggregate demand as real final consumption expenditure relative to the pre-displacement baseline. The Pass Threshold's figures apply in every scenario.

**Indicators.** Societal Poverty Line (as C1.1); national accounts final consumption expenditure; for a design, its own model of the three scenarios.

**Rationale.** This criterion alone disqualifies most existing frameworks. When machines replace humans at scale, systems dependent on wage labor for both income and aggregate demand collapse into deflationary spirals that no monetary policy can resolve.

**Derivation.** From N2 (Labor Non-Necessity) + N11 (Automation Compatibility)

**Pass Threshold, clause by clause** (all must be shown cleared for 1.0; protocol 2.3):

1. Poverty <8%
2. aggregate demand >85% baseline

**Note.** Apply it carefully; see also Appendix G for a documented case where a candidate evidentiary source (a companion simulation) could not actually be used to evaluate a system at this criterion's higher severities. the band texts stand.

**Anchor bands** (they describe; the Pass Threshold governs, protocol 4.6):

- **1.0** An explicit mechanism decoupling income/demand from employment, robust in the system's own design logic across displacement severities.
- **0.5** A buffer or partial mechanism exists but is not designed for or shown to survive the higher-severity scenarios.
- **0.0** No mechanism; system structurally requires wage labor for both income distribution and demand.
