[Research Hub: cco-ptf-simulation-replication.html] λ calibration status — open. The original range (0.001–0.008) is BLEI paper §4 Index IV's published specification. At that range, even λ's low end already produces ~89.8% advancement probability at this paper's own $2,281/month FBS worked example, and ~97.5% at the companion simulation's median-wage agent (~$3,680/month FBS under Full Integration) — meaning λ, whose stated purpose is to differentiate agents by individual capability, does little differentiating once FBS is solidly positive. v4.4 rescaled the simulation's own operational calibration to U(0.0001654, 0.0013233) — preserving the original 8× heterogeneity span (0.0013233/0.0001654 ≈ 8) while restoring meaningful variation in advancement probability across the model's actual FBS distribution. This is a provisional behavioral recalibration, not a dimensionally-forced conversion — FBS mixes a wage-derived term (which did change scale under v4.4's wage-scale unification) with fixed-dollar terms (BU face value, basic cost) that did not, so FBS as a whole does not scale by a single clean factor, and dividing λ by exactly the wage-conversion ratio is a chosen fix for saturation, not a unit identity. Passing the simulation's own validation suite (BU-monotonicity and five other internal-consistency checks) demonstrates the rescaled range restores useful behavioral heterogeneity — it does not establish that 0.0001654–0.0013233 are empirically correct real-world advancement probabilities; λ's empirical grounding remains open in both the original and rescaled form. The BLEI paper itself retains 0.001–0.008 as its published theoretical specification rather than being silently rewritten to match an evolving simulation calibration — see its own note at Index IV for the same discussion. A more interpretable reparameterization — reporting the half-saturation point FBS₅₀ = ln(2)/λ instead of a raw 1/USD coefficient — is a recommended future direction: the original range implies FBS₅₀ ≈ $87–$693/month (a high-capability agent needs only $87/month of residual bandwidth for even odds of advancing); the v4.4 range implies FBS₅₀ ≈ $524–$4,191/month, a substantially more meaningful spread given the FBS levels the model actually generates. The model's figure is the rise in basket poverty (adults below the cost of living) with today's safety net, the shock run minus the same run with no recession, over 500 paired seeds; the data's is the change from the year before the shock. In short: the direction is right (poverty rises in both shocks, as in the data), the size is too big (a rise of +6.86 points at the model's peak against +2.6 in the official rate) and the peak comes too late. The release row's cautious reading: of what the Source pays out (BU spent at face value plus conversion proceeds, less output that backs it), none is backed by new output (`a = 0`); counted output is ESP reinvestment (capital goods) and the whole project payout (`PROD.match 'market'`). Unmatched money `U = (1 − a) × (BU spent at face value + conversion paid − matched output)`; the programme's raises are taken as matched by output (`aw` 1) and a write-off at the wealth floor is unmet need, not money. The general price level moves with it: `P_G,t+1 = P_G,t × (1 + λ_G × U_t / Y_t)`, λ_G = 1, Y the year's aggregate cash income (quantity-theory benchmark, a logged placeholder swept in the testbed). Essentials prices move with demand above the no-programme capacity: `P_E,k = 1 + θ_k × (D − S)/S`, θ = 0 (food), 0.6 (housing), 0.5 (medical; unsourced placeholder), and are also multiplied by `P_G`.
